Position-dependent bias limits randomness extraction in public bit sequences from nine IBM quantum computers: a screened, false-discovery-controlled audit

Random bits taken from cloud quantum computers need health checks before use. We audited a public archive of 5340 bit strings from four circuit types on nine IBM devices, with reference sources from the same archive. A data screen removed 25 nearly constant strings from one circuit and 3 repeated files. Five statistical tests per string were combined per circuit with false-discovery-rate control. All nine devices failed: 33 of 36 circuits had at least one rejection, while five clean references passed and six biased generators were detected. The von Neumann extractor left 10 circuits rejected, 8 of them of the third circuit type. All nine circuits of that type, and none of the other 27, showed a significant dependence of the share of ones on bit position, with a period of four. Applying the extractor separately to the four position streams removed every rejection in all 36 circuits, keeping about 24.9 percent of the bits. This last step was chosen after seeing the data, and passing five basic tests does not certify randomness. Position structure should be checked before an extractor is trusted.
