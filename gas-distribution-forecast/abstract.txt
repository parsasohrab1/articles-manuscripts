Day-ahead forecasting of gas distribution offtake and the balancing margin it implies: an open-data study of three European zones

Balancing a gas network requires a forecast of distribution offtake and a margin for its error. Using only open data, this study forecasts daily physical exit flow at distribution points in the Netherlands (low-calorific gas), Belgium (high-calorific gas) and Italy, with data from the ENTSOG Transparency Platform and temperature from NASA POWER. Ridge regression, random forest and gradient boosting were refitted every month and tested on 2024 and 2025. The random forest had the lowest error in all three zones, with mean absolute percentage errors of 3.0 to 6.4 % and mean absolute errors 45.5 to 58.9 % below those of repeating the previous day. A margin set at the 95th percentile of the last 90 absolute errors averaged 8.8 to 15.8 % of mean demand for the random forest against 22.2 to 27.5 % for the previous-day rule, but covered only 89.9 to 91.6 % of days, short of the nominal 95 %. The accuracy relies on same-day observed temperature standing in for a weather forecast: using only past temperature, or learning demand one day later, raised the error substantially. The results are a benchmark on public data, not a validation in an operating control room.
