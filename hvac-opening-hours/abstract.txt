How much HVAC electricity in small public buildings falls outside opening hours? A seven-month measurement study with schedule-based upper bounds

Programmes that promise a fixed percentage saving from smarter HVAC scheduling need to know how much electricity is actually used when a building is closed. This study analyses open smart-switch measurements of HVAC units in a town hall and a public nursery in Fornes (Spain) from June to December 2025: 176 and 211 complete days, 253.0 and 345.1 kWh. Standby consumption below 20 W was 6.2 % and 2.3 % of the measured energy, and removing it outside a 07:00 to 15:00 weekday window would save only 4.8 % and 1.7 %. Switching off all load outside that window gives upper bounds of 45.4 % and 41.0 %, which fall to 13.3 % and 10.3 % if the window ends at 22:00. The result therefore depends mainly on the true opening hours, which the dataset does not record. For daily consumption, ridge degree-day and random forest models did not beat repeating the value of the previous week (test error 1.48 against 1.13 kWh/day in the town hall and 1.8 against 1.33 in the nursery). No control action was applied, so no realised saving is claimed.
